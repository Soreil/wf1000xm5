# Sony WF-1000XM5 driver disassembly and repair notes

This is a photo record of accessing and removing the earbud's driver. The pictures below are arranged in capture order. They document the disassembly and expose the driver connections, but do not establish that the earbud was reassembled or tested successfully.

## 1. Open the earbud and locate the driver

With the shell open, the back of the round driver is visible in its white carrier. A small flexible connection board runs across the driver and terminates at two solder joints. Red compound is visible around the edge of the driver.

![Opened earbud showing the back of the driver, its two solder joints and red compound around the edge](information/driver%20half.JPG)

## 2. Inspect the connections before disturbing them

The closer view shows the two soldered contacts on the connection board. Note their position and routing before lifting anything: the driver is both electrically connected here and mechanically retained in the shell.

![Closer view of the connection board and solder joints on the driver](information/driver%20half%20second.JPG)

## 3. Desolder the connection board

I used **solder braid** to remove solder from the two joints connecting the board to the driver, then moved the board aside. Avoid pulling on the board while either joint is still attached; the fine coil leads nearby are much more fragile than the board.

![Driver with its connecting board desoldered and moved aside](information/connection%20board%20driver%20desoldered.JPG)

## 4. Work through the retaining compound

The red goop around the driver did **not seem to soften much with heat**. **Isopropyl alcohol (IPA)** worked really well to weaken it, making it easier to remove mechanically. I used a **scalpel** to carefully cut and scrape away the adhesives around the perimeter, working away from the driver and its wiring rather than levering hard against them.

![Partly cleared red compound around the driver after applying IPA and removing material](information/Red%20goop%20removal%20attempt%20IPA.JPG)

## 5. Free the driver from the shell

Removing the red compound alone is not enough: **there is also glue underneath it holding the driver in place**. Continue releasing that underlying glue with the scalpel before trying to lift the driver. The shell and driver are shown at the stage of separation below; the loose driver is visible in the following photos.

![Earbud shell and driver during separation, with adhesive remnants visible at the edge](information/Driver%20removed%20from%20case.JPG)

## 6. Inspect the removed driver

The front face and diaphragm of the freed driver are exposed here. Keep the scalpel and loose adhesive away from this surface: damaging the diaphragm would be difficult to undo.

![Front face and diaphragm of the removed driver](information/Driver%20front%20face.JPG)

## 7. Protect the coil wires

The **final image shows one of the two extremely fragile bare copper wires leading to the driver coils**, protruding from the side of the removed driver. These wires can be severed by a slight tug or a misplaced scalpel stroke. Check their route before any further cleaning, soldering, or reassembly, and avoid putting tension on them.

![Side of the removed driver showing a very fine bare copper coil wire](information/Driver%20wiring%20%282%20wires%20like%20this%29%29.JPG)

**Takeaway:** Desolder the connection board with braid first; use IPA and careful scalpel work to release both the red compound and the glue beneath it. Treat the exposed copper coil wires as the most delicate part of the operation.
